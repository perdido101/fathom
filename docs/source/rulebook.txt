SECTION 01
WHAT YOU ARE DOING
Somewhere across the table is a fleet you cannot see. You have a hand of cards, a slow trickle of energy, and twenty-
four possible hulls to reason about. So do they. Sink everything first.
Fathom starts before the first shot, in two drafts. Ships pass between you in small hidden packs — you keep one, you burn one face
down, and what you burned nobody ever learns. Action cards pass the same way, into a hidden hand. By the time fleets are drawn, each
of you knows a great deal about what the other might have, and nothing for certain.
Then the hunt: fire into the dark, read the answers, spend energy on cards and ship abilities, and lie wherever the sea lets you. The fleet
itself fights back — every hull carries an ability, paid for in cubes or triggered by being found.
HOW YOU WIN
Sink every enemy hull. No points, no timer. The last fleet afloat takes it.

SETUP, IN ORDER
1 Draft ships. Shuffle all 24 hull cards. Deal packs of four: receiver keeps 1, burns 1 face down, passes 2; the other player keeps
1, burns 1. Alternate who receives each pack. Five packs → 5 hulls each, 10 burned, 4 never dealt. Keep your hulls hidden in
front of you.
2 Deal two identical seas. Shuffle the grid cards, deal the match size (below), and build the matching layout on the second board
with the duplicate cards. Same orientation — the printed coordinates must read the same way on both.
3 Flip two terrain cards. They apply to both players all match. If the pair conflicts or makes deployment impossible, discard one
and redraw.
4 Deploy. Both players draw their fleet on their slates simultaneously, then turn them face down. Rules overleaf.
5 Draft action cards. Same pack draft as ships: packs of four from the 50, keep one, burn one, pass two. Ten packs → 10 cards
each into a hidden hand, 20 burned, 10 undealt.
6 Finish. Each player takes a Basic Salvo face up. Cubes and discs in reach. Whoever last swam in the sea goes first.

THE TWO DRAFTS
Keep one. Burn one. Pass two. What you burn, nobody ever sees.
Both drafts use the same engine. A pack of four is dealt face down; you look privately, keep one card, put one on the burn pile face
down, and hand the remaining two across. Your opponent keeps one of those and burns the other. The next pack goes to them first.

SHIPS, THEN CARDS
Ships are drafted before the sea is dealt, so you build a fleet before knowing the terrain — then deploy after seeing it. Action cards
are drafted last, after deployment and after the terrain cards are face up, so your toolkit answers the actual match: if Blockade is in
play, detection is taxed and you draft accordingly.
THE BURN PILE
All burned cards share one face-down pile. Several action cards reach into it — Press Gang, Scuttlebutt, Refit — so a burned card is
not always gone forever. Burned hulls are, unless a terrain card says otherwise.
The audit. The whole game runs on answering honestly for your own board. When the last hull sinks, both slates turn face up
and get checked against the discs — every red disc on a drawn hull, every white disc on water. Ten seconds, every match, no
exceptions.

DEPLOYMENT
Draw a line through the cells each hull occupies on your slate and write its length beside it. Horizontal, vertical, or diagonal — all
eight orientations are legal. Then turn the slate face down and leave it there until the audit.
Hulls may not overlap and may not run off the board.
Hulls may touch — adjacent hulls read as one long ship until suddenly they don't.
No cell may sit on reef. Every other symbol is legal, including wreckage.
Mark mines and similar deployment effects with an X on the slate.
Diagonals change the hunt. A hit means eight neighbours to check, not four. Expect longer matches, and draft detection
accordingly — or agree to orthogonal-only for a faster evening.

SECTION 06
TAKING A TURN
EARN SPEND REPORT
+2, straighten cards cards + ship abilities discs and answers
01 EARN
Take 2 cubes. Straighten cards you turned sideways on your previous turn. Tick down any timed effects.
02 SPEND
In any order, as long as you can pay: play action cards from your hand, and activate ship abilities by paying their ACT cost and flipping
the hull card face up. Every cube you earn from a hit arrives immediately and can be spent this same turn — a cheap probe
funding a big card is the game's signature move.
Action cards go face up, turned sideways. They straighten the turn after next and are reusable — except ONCE cards, which turn
face down forever.
Ship abilities reveal that the hull is in your fleet, never where. The card stays face up. ONCE abilities turn sideways when spent.
REACT abilities are free, automatic, and fire even on their owner's off-turn. They flip themselves.
Basic Salvo: fire one cell, free, once per turn, never sideways. You can always act.
Passing is legal. It is also how you fall behind.
03 REPORT
For every cell you fired at, place a disc white side up on their board. They check their slate and flip red anything that struck a hull.
Counts, reveals and other answers are spoken aloud; mark revealed information with detection markers so nobody has to memorise it.
ANNOUNCING SINKS
When a hull's last cell dies, its owner announces its length — "four sunk" — never its name. Names surface at the audit.
Exception: a hull that dies inside fog sinks in silence; the owner says only "hit."
No caps. Play as many cards and abilities per turn as you can afford, and bank cubes without limit. The economy is the only
brake — and a player who hoards is a player not earning hit bonuses.
